Quick guide

Is it an emergency?

Start at the top. The first match is what to do.

  1. 1

    Call 911 now and get out

    • Smoke, flames or a sounding smoke alarm: get out, stay out, call 911.
    • A downed or low power line: stay away, keep others and pets away, call 911, then your utility.
  2. 2

    Stop using it and call a licensed electrician now

    • Sparks from an outlet.
    • A burning or rubbery smell from an outlet, switch or appliance. Call 911 if there is smoke or heat.
    • Warm, discolored, cracked or broken outlets or switches.
    • A tingle or shock when you touch an appliance, sink or fixture.

EV chargers and backup power

Two projects people ask about most. Done to current electrical code.

Level 2 EV charging at home

Level 2 runs on 240 volts, the same as a dryer or oven, and adds roughly 25 to 40 miles of range per hour of charging.

  • It needs its own dedicated circuit.
  • The circuit is sized at 125% of the charger’s rating, so a 40-amp charger needs a 50-amp circuit.
  • Plug-in (NEMA 14-50 outlet) or hardwired. Chargers of 48 amps and up are usually hardwired.
  • Outdoor installation is safe with outdoor-rated equipment.
  • 100-amp service is often enough. A load calculation tells you whether your panel can handle it or needs an upgrade.

Standby generators and transfer switches

A generator must connect through a transfer switch, never a wall outlet. Without one, power can backfeed into utility lines and seriously injure or kill line workers.

  • With an automatic transfer switch, the generator is sized to carry everything the switch transfers, unless the loads are managed.
  • We pull permits when the city requires them.
